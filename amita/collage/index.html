Hello, I’m Amita

I’m from Indore. I started freelancing in December 2018 — coupon articles, daily deadlines, an editor’s initials in every filename. Nobody’s showreel starts there. Mine did, and it taught me to file on time, which turns out to be half the job.

Mumbai came next: agency side, at TeamPumpkin, 2021 to 2023. Insurance, cookware, lending, steel. That’s where I learned to write for a category that doesn’t want to be written about.

Since 2023 I’ve run my own freelance practice — retainers, not gigs. Skincare, luxury, jewellery, tea, restaurants, a psychotherapist’s brochure, a diagnostic centre’s growth plan.

Now I’m at Eventually Co, and the job is bigger than copy. I write the content strategy — positioning, buckets, platform plans — and I’m on set directing the shoots that come out of it: doctors doing piece-to-camera, hotels, restaurants, founder portraits. Writing the script and then standing behind the camera while someone says it out loud changes how you write the next one.

And I’m doing a PGDM at Goa Institute of Management, in Operations and Finance. Not a career change — a modifier. I’d rather be the content person who can read the P&L than the one who leaves the room when it comes up.

Every brief is a chase

Why

Every brand has a business goal it can’t say out loud.

The brief is never the problem. What the brief left out is.

So I go find that first.

What

Then I make the thing that says it.

Campaign lines. Content calendars. Video scripts. Strategy documents. A founder’s LinkedIn.

Sometimes all six for the same client.

How

One decision per piece, and I’ll show you the decision.

What we chose. What we killed. What the client sent back.

The work is downstream of that.

Strategy, not just posts

Wealth managementHospital fitness clubEventually Co2025 to confirm — client sign-off to name; until then, published anonymised

WhyTwo clients, same request: give us posts. Neither had a reason for anyone to follow them. A calendar can’t fix that. You have to decide what the brand is for first.

WhatTwo strategy documents. For a wealth-management firm: a positioning line, five named content buckets — each with its own job, not just a topic — a per-platform plan for LinkedIn, Instagram, podcast and video, and a full podcast format with three episode types, runtimes and a repurposing chain down to reels. For a hospital’s fitness club: goals, seven buckets each opening with a stated objective, then the executions — post titles, carousel scripts, a lead-gen offer, geo-targeting logic — and a closing section on what the whole thing is supposed to earn the business.

HowThe decision in both was to lead with the thing the client thought was boring. The fitness club kept selling equipment; every gym sells equipment. It sits inside a hospital. So the line became where fitness meets medical trust, and the doctors went into the content plan, not the footer. The wealth firm kept publishing market updates; so does everyone. The bucket that earns the fee is the one where the advisor shows how he reviews a portfolio.

My partWrote both strategy documents end to end.

Resultto confirm — adoption: was either deck approved, and did it run Proxy: both documents descend from goal → bucket → objective → execution, and each bucket carries its rationale before its examples. That structure is the deliverable.

Hinglish that sells

Cookware + lendingTeamPumpkin2021–23

WhyTwo categories nobody brings up at a party: a pressure cooker and a loan app. Both were posting product features into a feed that only rewards recognition.

WhatCulture-first social for both. For the cookware brand: kitchen truths instead of kitchen shots. For the lending app, running as an IPL team’s digital partner: money worries mapped to lines people already know by heart.

HowHinglish only when the insight is actually cultural. In India we don’t ask khana khaya, we say khana kha ke jana — that sentence isn’t a translation, it’s the whole insight about how Indian homes show care. Same with Aamdani athanni phir bhi kharcha rupaiya? — a loan app can’t lecture you about money, but a phrase your family already uses can. And the one I’d still defend in a room: on International Men’s Day, for a cookware brand — a cooker is supposed to be strong; men are allowed to be soft. Cultural insight, product truth, one line.

My partWrote the copy inside the agency account team. Not media, not design, not the RCB partnership deal. to confirm — which posts were her concepts versus the account team’s

How I work

1

I read before I write.

Audience, competitors, the client’s own literature, the last six months of their feed. For skincare that meant ingredient decks. For steel it meant a plant’s vocabulary. The joke comes after the research, never instead of it.

2

I decide, then I make.

Buckets before posts. Objectives before ideas. A page framework before website copy. If I can’t say what a piece is for in one line, it doesn’t get made.

3

I use AI, and I’ll tell you where.

It’s good for research passes, first drafts and reformatting. It is bad at the specific thing I’m paid for: knowing that khana kha ke jana is an insight and not a translation. So the drafts can be assisted. The judgment and the joke are mine, and I say which is which.

4

I keep the feedback where you can see it.

On the skincare retainer I logged 172 numbered rows: concept, first draft, the brand’s note, the revision, the approved version. When a client said a peg wouldn’t land, that went in the sheet too. You should be able to see me get corrected. That’s the fastest way to know how I’ll work with you.
